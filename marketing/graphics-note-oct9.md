# Graphics — round 6, needed Fri 9 Oct

Last round sold what the Journal *is*. This round is about what it **tells you** — and it
comes from something that actually happened rather than a hunch.

Same dark/gold direction as batch 3–5. No new look needed.

**Deadline: Friday 9 October.** If only some land, prioritise #1 and #2.

---

## Why this round exists

Someone found us through a comment, DM'd, and asked two questions:

1. *How do you log your trades?*
2. *How do you read it?*

Last round answered the first one well — logging, phone, imports, all covered. **Nothing we
have answers the second.** Every graphic so far shows the calendar as a nice-looking object.
None of them show what a trader learns from it.

There's a second reason. Of the people who own the Journal, almost none have logged
anything. That isn't a persuasion problem — they're already convinced enough to sign up.
It's that starting feels like admin with no payoff. Graphics that show the payoff *before*
the work is the gap.

So: **stop showing the product. Show what it tells you.**

---

## The five pieces

### 1. The number with its denominator
The most important thing we do, and we've never made a graphic of it.

> **68% win rate.**
> *Over 22 trades.*

The second line in smaller, quieter type — but present, and clearly attached. The whole
point is that we show the sample size next to the percentage when nobody else does.

If there's room underneath:

> Small sample sizes flatter you. We'd rather you knew.

This is our most honest thing. Give it the most design care in the round.

### 2. What your calendar is telling you
Not a pretty month grid — a month grid **being read**.

Take a plausible month, mostly green with three or four red days, and annotate it the way a
person would: a line or arrow pointing at the red days with a short callout.

> *Three of your four losing days were Fridays.*

Then underneath:

> **You don't notice that in your head. You notice it here.**

Made-up numbers, and make the pattern genuinely visible in the grid so the callout is
earned rather than asserted.

### 3. Your curve, in plain words
An equity curve with two labels on it, nothing more:

> **This is your money over time.**
> *And this dip is the worst it ever got — your drawdown.*

Most traders have heard "max drawdown" and never had it pointed at. One graphic, one
pointing finger.

### 4. Empty → full
A two-panel before/after. Left: an empty journal, one lonely trade. Right: the same journal
with a filled calendar and a curve.

> **One CSV. Four months of trades.**
> Bybit · MEXC · Binance · MT4 · MT5 · cTrader

This one is aimed squarely at the people who signed up and stopped. The message is *you
don't have to build this up slowly.*

### 5. A reply card for Rose
Different job from the others: a **single square image** she can drop into a comment thread
when someone asks what Porfilr is. Most of these accounts have DMs closed, so a comment is
the only channel.

Must work at thumbnail size and with no caption:

> **Log your trades. See what they're telling you.**
> Free for your first 15 · porfilr.com/trading-journal

Plain, no hard sell. It gets used in someone else's replies, so it should look like an
answer, not an ad.

## Standing rules

- **Never "verified", and no investor framing.** Numbers are self-reported and the product
  says so. Being straight about that is the pitch, not a caveat to it.
- **No real user data** — no names, no real P&L, no screenshots of anyone's account.
- **Made-up numbers must be plausible.** A mixed month with real losses. No fantasy runs of
  green — this audience can smell a fake equity curve instantly, and it would cost us more
  than the graphic gains.
- Every claim is something the product does today. If a line reads stronger than the truth,
  send it to me rather than shipping it.
